## Editorial position

Generic schedule content already covers weekly game totals, off-night leaders, back-to-backs, and streamer names. Cracked Ice should lead with questions that require its league-aware schedule engine:

- How many scheduled games can this specific roster actually start?
- Which similarly ranked player fits this roster or draft build better?
- How does changing the fantasy playoff cutoff reverse the answer?
- Which teams complement an existing positional anchor instead of merely having many off-nights?

The recurring voice should be: one counterintuitive claim, the calculation that proves it, the limitation, and a tool readers can use with their own settings.

## Priority launch pieces

### 1. The 84-game illusion

**Hook:** Every team plays 84 games, but the best two-team schedule pairing creates 27 more usable dates than the worst.

**Why it works:** Timely first season of the 84-game schedule; immediately distinguishes scheduled games from fantasy starts.

**Asset:** Off-Night Bible plus best/worst pairing graphic.

### 2. The date that changes your draft board

**Hook:** San Jose ranks first across one playoff window, then falls to two games and zero off-nights in the final NHL week.

**Why it works:** Demonstrates why generic “best playoff schedule” lists can mislead; naturally sends readers to league date settings.

**Asset:** Three-window ranking flip graphic.

### 3. Two premium RWs, then what?

**Hook:** You were fortunate enough to land Nikita Kucherov and David Pastrnak. The third right wing can have a slightly lower scoring rate and still create substantially more usable starts.

**Why it works:** Starts with recognizable elite players, then moves schedule math to the realistic point where it should influence a draft: a later choice inside a comparable tier.

**Asset:** Kucherov + Pastrnak roster header, two third-RW candidates, and a usable-start comparison card. Keep the separate 17-date TBL team-partner diagnostic clearly labeled as a one-slot schedule extreme rather than an exact third-RW projection.

### 4. Stop drafting off-night teams; draft complements

**Hook:** Two individually favorable schedules can still collide. Team rank is not roster fit.

**Why it works:** Contrarian correction to the most common off-night advice and a direct expression of Cracked Ice's core math.

**Asset:** Four-team example showing team rank versus pair rank.

### 5. The games after your championship do not exist

**Hook:** Rest-of-season totals silently reward NHL games played after a fantasy league has ended.

**Why it works:** Solves a real draft and in-season valuation error discussed during product development.

**Asset:** Before/after cutoff comparison with “unusable after season” games called out.

## Weekly recurring formats

1. **One move, three windows:** Compare the same pickup for next week, next two weeks, and fantasy playoffs.
2. **Anchor audit:** Pick one widely rostered player and identify the three cleanest schedule complements at the same position.
3. **Sunday night receipt:** Review which Monday recommendations produced usable starts, not merely NHL games.
4. **The congestion trap:** Highlight a four-game team whose games mostly land on already-full nights.
5. **Goalie B2B board:** Show back-to-back opportunity while explicitly separating opportunity from confirmed starts.
6. **Playoff-window flip:** One team that gains or loses value when the championship cutoff moves by seven days.

## Share-first formats

- **A/B decision card:** Two players, league scoring label, exact dates, projected usable points, and one-sentence verdict.
- **Roster collision map:** Position row with green usable games and red conflicts, designed to invite “what would you change?” replies.
- **Schedule receipt:** Pre-week recommendation beside post-week actual starts, creating accountability.
- **One-chart myth:** A common claim such as “four games is always better than three” tested against actual lineup room.

## Guardrails

- Never call a player a free agent without provider, screenshot, or user-supplied availability provenance.
- Never present the editorial reference FPPG as the user's scoring.
- Always state the exact date window and off-night definition.
- Schedule is a tiebreaker and opportunity model, not a substitute for talent, role, health, or news.
- Use current-team data and verify player examples immediately before publication.
